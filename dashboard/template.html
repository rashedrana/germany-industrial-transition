Q18 · Small multiples — the same story, ten shapes (manufacturing share 1991–2024, uniform 0–35% scale)

Germany, Italy and the EU drop steeply in the 1990s then plateau; France slides almost without pause; China arcs to a 2006 peak of 32.0%; Poland alone draws a U-turn, dipping to 14.1% in 2002 before recovering.

Data source

World Bank — World Development Indicators (WDI)
databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators
Key series: manufacturing value added (% of GDP, NV.IND.MANF.ZS) and in constant 2015 US$ (NV.IND.MANF.KD), industry employment shares (SL.IND.EMPL.ZS), GDP per person employed (SL.GDP.PCAP.EM.KD) and high-tech exports (TX.VAL.TECH.MF.ZS).
Coverage
10-country panel (Germany + 9 comparison countries/aggregates), 1991–2024 · 183-country cross-section for 2023 · 10,732 fact rows in the Power BI model (dashboard/powerbi/data/). Known WDI gaps (China from 2004, US share to 2021) are shown as gaps, never filled.
Consistency
Every figure on this page matches the companion scientific paper “Still the Workshop of Europe? Germany's Industrial Transition, 1991–2024” number for number — both are built from the same WDI extract in the project repo.
Built by Rashed Rana as the interactive companion to the research paper “Still the Workshop of Europe? Germany's Industrial Transition, 1991–2024” — the full analysis, SQL queries and paper live in the GitHub project. This web edition mirrors the Power BI report (star-schema model, DAX measures and page layout in the repo under dashboard/powerbi/). Data: World Bank World Development Indicators, accessed 2026. Industrial employment and value added per worker are derived as documented in the paper (labour force × employment rate × industry share).
